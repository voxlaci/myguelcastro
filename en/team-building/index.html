What it is — and what it isn't

It is a hands-on experience. During the session the team makes music together — it does not watch a performance or a lecture. Starting from rhythm, voice and listening, the group builds, from scratch, something no single person could do alone. Complexity increases gradually and the final result is only possible with real cooperation.

Not a conference.

Nobody sits and listens. Everyone takes part.

Not a music lesson.

No theory is taught. The team works through music.

No need to know how to sing.

Designed for people with no musical experience.

It is a team experience.

Communication, listening and trust tested in real time.

What happens in the session

Through the experience, participants:

  • move, experiment and listen to each other;
  • communicate and respond to others, in pairs and small groups;
  • learn simple rhythms and melodic lines and improvise on them;
  • coordinate several functions at the same time;
  • build, layer by layer, a collective result.

Each step adds a new demand. When a team can hold its own part and hear the others, the result appears — and everyone feels it immediately.

What it develops

Communication & listening

Listening before responding; adjusting to others in real time.

Trust

Risking your own voice in a group and being welcomed changes how colleagues relate.

Cooperation & coordination

More than one function at once, synchronised with the rest of the team.

Leadership & individual responsibility

Each person holds their part; leadership moves around the group.

Creativity & adaptation

Improvise, test, correct and try again without fear of mistakes.

Belonging & collective building

A result the team recognises as its own and remembers for months.

International teams

Available in English and Portuguese. The format works especially well with international teams because much of the communication happens through demonstration, music, rhythm, gesture and non-verbal interaction — it does not depend on everyone sharing the same first language.

The session can be delivered entirely in English, entirely in Portuguese, or bilingually.

Logistics

Myguel Santos e Castro travels to the company's premises or the event venue. The requirements are simple:

  • a room with space for people to move;
  • movable chairs — preferably no tables;
  • a whiteboard and markers;
  • printing of a few materials sent in advance.

The facilitator brings a keyboard. A stage and a large sound system are usually not required.

Myguel Santos e Castro travels anywhere in Portugal — Lisbon, Oeiras, Cascais, Sintra, Porto and the rest of the country — and internationally. All that is needed is a room with space to move and movable chairs.

What activity works with international teams?

Music team building works well with multicultural teams because much of the communication happens through demonstration, rhythm, gesture and music. The session can be in English or Portuguese.

Do participants need to know how to sing?

No. The experience is designed for people with no musical experience. It starts with rhythm and listening and complexity increases gradually.
